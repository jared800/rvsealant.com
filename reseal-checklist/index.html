RV roof reseal checklist

From RVSealant.com. Every step below comes from the sealant makers' own labels and spec pages. Your product label always wins. Print this page

Safety first. Not every RV roof is made to be walked on. Check your owner's manual before you climb up, use a proper ladder, and work with a helper. Lap sealants are solvent-based; Dicor's label calls for fresh air and warns against heat, sparks and flame.

1. Before you buy anything

  • ☐ Know your roof: EPDM (rubber), TPO, fiberglass, or aluminum/metal. Check the owner's manual, a roof label near the ladder or edge, or ask your dealer with your VIN. White EPDM and white TPO can look alike, so don't guess from color.
  • ☐ EPDM, TPO or PVC roof edges, vents and screw heads: a lap sealant made for membranes (Dicor lists EPDM, TPO and PVC for its lap sealants).
  • ☐ Flat joints get self-leveling lap sealant. Vertical or sloped edges and termination rails get non-sag (non-leveling).
  • ☐ Do NOT put Geocel Pro Flex RV on EPDM or TPO (Geocel says not to). Do NOT put Sashco Through the Roof! on EPDM (Sashco says not to).
  • ☐ Long seams or tears: seam tape such as EternaBond RoofSeal instead of a caulk bead.
  • ☐ Tools: caulking gun, plastic scraper, brushes, clean rags, painter's tape, and a cleaner your roof maker approves.

2. Prep

  • ☐ Inspect the whole roof. Mark cracked, lifted or missing sealant around vents, skylights, the A/C shroud, antenna, ladder mounts, edges and seams.
  • ☐ Clean off all dirt, loose paint, rust, oil and grease, then let the surface dry (Dicor).
  • ☐ Scrape off loose, cracked or peeling sealant.
  • ☐ Remove any silicone down to a clean surface. Several makers say their products won't bond over silicone.

3. Weather check

  • ☐ Seam tape: apply between 40°F and 150°F; below 40°F, EternaBond says to use its EternaPrime primer.
  • ☐ Cold day: warm lap sealant tubes to room temperature first (Dicor).

4. Apply

  • ☐ Seams and tears: tape onto a clean, dry surface and roll it down firmly.
  • ☐ Bead the lap sealant over the full joint and every screw head.
  • ☐ Windows, vents and skylight flanges: the primary seal under the flange is a butyl sealant (Dicor lists its gunnable butyl for this).

5. Cure and re-check

  • ☐ Dicor's cure guide at 50–70°F: skins in about 5 minutes, waterproof in about 4 hours, 80% cured at 48 hours, fully cured at 30 days.
  • ☐ Look again after the first rain or the first trip, then on a regular schedule.
  • ☐ If the membrane itself is chalky, cracked or worn across the roof, a spot reseal won't fix it. That's a full recoat job, or an inspection at an RV service center.
