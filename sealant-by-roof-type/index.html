RV sealant by roof type

Updated 2026-10-20

The roof material decides what will bond. Here is what each manufacturer says its product works on, and where it says not to use it.

On this page
  1. EPDM (rubber)
  2. TPO
  3. Fiberglass
  4. Aluminum and metal
  5. Not sure which roof you have?

EPDM (rubber) roofs

Use a lap sealant rated for EPDM around edges, vents and screw heads: Dicor's self-leveling (501LS) and non-sag (551LS) lap sealants both list EPDM. For seams and tears, EternaBond RoofSeal tape lists EPDM. For a full recoat, Tough Tech lists EPDM rubber for Tough Roof RV.

Avoid on EPDM (per the makers): Geocel Pro Flex RV and Sashco Through the Roof! Both say not to use them on EPDM rubber membranes.

Check Dicor self-leveling priceMaker's spec page

TPO roofs

Dicor lists TPO as compatible with both its self-leveling and non-sag lap sealants and with its gunnable butyl sealant. EternaBond RoofSeal and Tough Roof RV also list TPO.

Avoid on TPO (per the maker): Geocel says not to use Pro Flex RV on TPO.

Check Dicor non-sag priceMaker's spec page

Fiberglass roofs and caps

Geocel Pro Flex RV is made for fiberglass, aluminum, wood and vinyl joints, so it suits fiberglass caps, trim and moldings. Dicor lap sealants also adhere to fiberglass, and EternaBond lists fiberglass for seam repairs.

Check Geocel Pro Flex RV priceMaker's spec page

Aluminum and metal roofs

Sashco lists metal RV roofs, flashing, vent pipes and skylights for Through the Roof!, and says it sticks to wet surfaces. Geocel Pro Flex RV and Dicor lap sealants both list aluminum. EternaBond lists aluminum and galvanized steel.

Check Sashco Through the Roof! priceMaker's spec page

Not sure which roof you have?

  • Check your owner's manual or the RV manufacturer's spec sheet for your model year.
  • Look for a roof label near the ladder or roof edge; many RVs have one.
  • Ask your RV dealer or service center with your VIN.

We don't recommend guessing from color alone. White EPDM and white TPO can look similar.
